STATE DATA SPOTLIGHT · TEXAS

Texas: A State-Level Crime Data Example

Texas publishes immigration-status-linked conviction and arrest data that can provide useful context. This is a secondary state case study, not a nationwide estimate or substitute for the national case explorer. Methodology and classification limitations still apply.

NATIONAL DATA GAP

There is no comprehensive public national database tracking serious crimes by unlawful immigration status.

Federal and state systems collect different pieces of the picture, but no single publicly accessible dataset reliably counts homicide, sexual assault, fatal DUI crashes and other serious offenses nationwide by a person's immigration status at the time of the incident. That is a major transparency gap. The figures below are from Texas only—not a national total. Texas DPS methodology and data

What these records establish

Verified: Texas documents convictions associated with individuals whose unlawful presence was identified through DHS Secure Communities.

Separate population: Texas also reports additional individuals identified while incarcerated. Those convictions are not added to the headline counts.

Not established: A national total, a nationwide undocumented-immigrant crime rate, or a comparison with U.S.-citizen offending rates.

Important gap: National fatal DUI crash totals cannot currently be reliably attributed by immigration status. Drug-offense convictions also do not measure fentanyl entering the U.S.

Trends and comparable rates

Multi-year Texas offense-specific series and consistent population-adjusted comparisons are not yet published here. The figures above are cumulative, not annual trends. We will add dated series only after verifying comparable definitions and denominators.

CHILD PROTECTION / FEDERAL OVERSIGHT / DOCUMENTED FAILURES

Unaccounted-For Migrant Children: What Happened After Release?

Federal inspectors have documented gaps in tracking children after custody, sponsor screening and safety follow-up. These are distinct categories: missed court hearings, incomplete address records, inadequate safety checks and confirmed abuse or trafficking are not interchangeable. Each finding below is tied to its original oversight report.

DHS INSPECTOR GENERAL · AUG 2024
32,000+

Missed immigration hearings

DHS OIG reported more than 32,000 unaccompanied children failed to appear for scheduled immigration hearings during FY2019–FY2023. This does not mean all were confirmed missing or trafficked.

Read original DHS OIG alert
DHS INSPECTOR GENERAL · MAR 2025
31,000+

Incomplete release addresses

More than 31,000 children's release addresses were blank, undeliverable or lacked apartment numbers in records reviewed for FY2019–FY2023. This is an address-quality finding, not an additional count of missing children.

Read original DHS OIG audit
HHS INSPECTOR GENERAL · FEB 2024
16%

Missing sponsor-check documentation

In an HHS OIG sample of children released in early 2021, 16% of case files lacked documentation for one or more required sponsor safety checks. This is a sample finding, not a national percentage of unsafe placements.

Read original HHS OIG evaluation
HHS INSPECTOR GENERAL · OCT 2026
~1,800

Children in safety-concern reports

During January–June 2024, the ORR call center received nearly 2,000 reports of potential safety concerns involving approximately 1,800 released children. These were reports of potential concerns, not 1,800 confirmed trafficking victims.

Read original HHS OIG evaluation
Why the categories matter: Some children could not be located through immigration-court processes; others had incomplete records or reported safety concerns. These groups may overlap, and none should be added together into a single “missing children” total. Confirmed exploitation cases require separate investigation and sourcing.

Sources & Methodology

2026 data: FY2026 preliminary federal sentencing offense counts are available through June 30, 2026, and are displayed separately from final annual data.

Update workflow: Monthly automated source monitoring is scheduled for the first day of each month, starting December 1, 2026. It produces a research report; it does not automatically modify this HTML or publish a website update. Reviewed data must be incorporated and redeployed.

Population: The federal series covers all non-U.S. citizens, including people lawfully present. It cannot be relabeled as exclusively undocumented-immigrant crime.

Measure: Federal sentencing cases in a fiscal year; not the number of criminal incidents that occurred that year, nor unique offenders nationwide.

Duplication: No federal and state totals have been combined. This avoids treating overlapping prosecution systems as mutually exclusive.

DUI: DUI is primarily prosecuted at state/local levels and cannot be reliably assessed from this federal series.

Cluster analysis: A defensible case-level sample is needed before estimating themes with K-means or categorical clustering. This dashboard does not fabricate clusters.
