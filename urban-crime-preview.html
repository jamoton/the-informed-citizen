Hispanic victimization: what the national survey records

In the same 2024 BJS survey, an estimated 274,230 violent incidents involving Hispanic victims were attributed to offenders perceived as Hispanic, compared with 334,770 attributed to White offenders and 288,130 to Black offenders. The survey also estimated 226,920 incidents with unknown offender race or ethnicity.

These are nonfatal violent incidents, not homicides, and estimates carry sampling uncertainty. They do not support a claim that most violence against Hispanic victims was committed by Hispanic offenders.

Why it matters: A national decline can coexist with severe harm in particular communities. The question for every administration is whether residents are safer, and what the evidence shows about its decisions.

Four checks before comparing cities

01
Choose the same offense definition

Homicide, robbery, assault, burglary, and DUI should not be combined indiscriminately.

02
Confirm reporting coverage

Verify complete reporting months and the agency's jurisdiction.

03
Normalize population

Use rates per 100,000 residents when appropriate, alongside counts.

04
Separate correlation from causation

Changes in enforcement, reporting, population, or policy do not alone establish what caused crime to rise or fall.

How to interpret the atlas

Continuous-update policy: Preserve historical annual data and append new years when verified source releases become available. Never label older data as current. Mark partial-year counts YTD with cutoff dates and distinguish provisional from finalized figures. Trend views should offer counts and population-adjusted rates, comparable time windows, and source-specific release dates. Do not annualize partial-year counts without explaining assumptions.

A
Verified facts

The national 2025 FBI estimates shown here are sourced. The Chicago and Milwaukee case files cite original or attributed local sources; the other city choices do not assert offense counts.

B
What “urban” means

A geographic lens, not a separate FBI crime classification. City police jurisdictions and metropolitan areas are different boundaries.

C
What is excluded

No unsupported city rankings, inferred demographics, or causal claims. DUI is not automatically counted as FBI violent crime.

D
Human review

New city comparisons require source verification, reporting completeness checks, and editorial approval before publication.
